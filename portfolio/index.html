Spiritual & Professional Philosophy

The Foundation of Discipleship: Humility and Love

Throughout my personal and professional journey, I have realized that technical expertise is only part of what makes a successful IT professional. True impact comes from how we treat others. For this portfolio, I have chosen to focus on two Christlike attributes that profoundly shape my interactions: humility and love.

The Essence of Love

Love is the driving force behind all meaningful service. In a recent General Conference, Elder Ronald A. Rasband taught that "Jesus Christ is the perfect embodiment of love. Love is the centerpiece of His gospel and the essence of being like Him." He further defined what this looks like in action, stating, "Love is not stepping back... Love is being interested in their lives, their successes, and their challenges. Love is being there with arms outstretched and a cheerful countenance".

I have found that in the workplace, love translates to genuine investment in the success of my team and end-users. It means looking past my own tasks to ensure others feel supported and capable.

Humility and Long-Suffering in Practice

Love naturally fosters humility. Humility is not thinking less of yourself, but thinking of yourself less; it is the willingness to step down from a place of expertise to walk patiently beside someone else.

I experienced a profound test of this during a recent project where I was assigned to train a colleague who had zero technical background on our new network management system. During the first week, he was overwhelmed. Steps we had practiced the day before were forgotten by the next morning, and his frustration was palpable. My initial instinct, driven by deadlines, was to take the mouse and simply do it for him.

However, relying on the principles of long-suffering and humility, I realized that taking over would serve my pride, not his growth. Instead, I took a breath and reset my approach. I broke the system down into bite-sized, visual steps. We went over the same fundamental processes repeatedly for two weeks. When he apologized for being "slow," I offered a cheerful countenance and reassured him that we were a team. Exercising that level of patience required me to swallow my ego and prioritize his learning over my convenience. Eventually, it clicked. Seeing the genuine pride on his face when he independently resolved his first ticket was far more rewarding than any solo technical achievement I've had.

Conclusion

These attributes are not just Sunday principles; they are everyday professional tools. By striving to embody the Savior’s love and practicing humility in my daily work, I am not only building better technical solutions, but I am helping to build up the people around me. This, ultimately, is the kind of professional I am dedicated to becoming.

Systems Architecture & Case Study

Technical Analysis

The Context and Incident

During the design and deployment of the Promised Land Linux data center in Independence, MO, I was tasked with establishing the file sharing and storage infrastructure. The initial configuration utilized broad, group-restricted share file access. While this method was fast to deploy and technically functional, it presented a significant ethical concern regarding data privacy and the over-exposure of sensitive corporate and personal information.

The Professional Dilemma

The dilemma was whether to proceed with a standard, easy-to-manage group access model—which is common but inherently insecure—or to invest the extra time and technical overhead required to build a strictly governed access system. As an IT professional, I had to balance the company's need for operational efficiency with the ethical mandate to protect individuals' information from unauthorized internal access.

The Action and Resolution

I chose to prioritize security and ethical compliance over convenience. I completely overhauled the access model, replacing the static group shares with strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Furthermore, I implemented comprehensive audit logging to track exactly who was accessing sensitive data and when. To ensure the system could handle this securely as it grew, I replaced static disk partitions with Logical Volume Management (LVM) for dynamic, secure storage scalability. Finally, I conducted an audit to remove unnecessary personal information from active, easily accessible files.

Ethical and Spiritual Reflection

This architectural decision was driven by the core values of honesty, responsibility, and fairness. Ethical IT is not just about reacting to breaches; it is about proactively designing systems that respect and protect user privacy from the ground up. By transitioning access from public to strict control, I ensured that our technical solutions were seamlessly integrated with ethical principles, proving that data protection is a fundamental responsibility, not an afterthought.

Peer Review & Continuous Improvement

Professional Peer Review & Improvement

Peer Evaluation & Commendations

During the peer review process, Nsokeunam Linus David provided valuable insights regarding my Linux Deployment Proposal for the Promised Land data center. He noted that the project demonstrated a strong technical understanding of server deployment, specifically praising the integration of file, web, database, and backup servers alongside critical security configurations like SELinux and firewalls. Nsokeunam also highlighted my transition to Logical Volume Management (LVM) as a smart technical refinement for future storage flexibility.

From an ethical standpoint, he commended the project's focus on data privacy. Nsokeunam pointed out that implementing strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and audit logging successfully connected technical security measures with the responsible, ethical handling of sensitive information.

Constructive Criticism

The primary area identified for improvement was the depth of documentation surrounding access and backups. Nsokeunam suggested providing more specific details on exactly how the RBAC roles would be assigned and what the process would be for reviewing the audit logs. Additionally, he recommended clarifying how the backup servers would protect sensitive information and how access to that backup data would be controlled.

Implementation of Feedback

Based on Nsokeunam's constructive feedback, I made the following adjustments to refine my final project:

  • RBAC and Audit Clarity: I expanded the documentation to include a clear framework for how RBAC roles are assigned based on the principle of least privilege, and I outlined a schedule for routine audit log reviews.
  • Backup Security: I added a section explicitly detailing the encryption protocols used for the backup servers and defined strict, isolated access controls for backup retrieval to ensure sensitive information remains protected even in storage.
